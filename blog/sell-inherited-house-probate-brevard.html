What Is Probate, and Why Does It Matter in Florida?

Probate is the legal process through which a deceased person's estate is settled. In Florida, this means validating the will (if one exists), identifying and inventorying assets, paying off debts and taxes, and distributing what remains to the rightful heirs. Real estate almost always goes through probate unless it was held in a living trust, owned jointly with rights of survivorship, or had a transfer-on-death deed in place.

Florida has two main types of probate administration:

  • Formal Administration: The standard process for larger estates or complex situations. It requires a personal representative (executor), court supervision, and typically takes 6 to 12 months — sometimes longer if disputes arise.
  • Summary Administration: A faster, simplified process available when the estate's total value is under $75,000 (excluding exempt property) or when the decedent has been deceased for more than two years. This can be completed in 1 to 3 months.

In Brevard County, where many longtime residents bought homes decades ago for a fraction of today's values, even modest properties can push an estate over the summary administration threshold. A home in Rockledge purchased in 1995 for $85,000 might be worth $350,000 today — meaning formal administration is often required.

Can You Sell a House During Probate in Brevard County?

Yes — but with important conditions. In Florida, real estate cannot be sold by the heirs alone until probate is complete and title has been transferred. However, the personal representative (appointed by the court) has the authority to sell probate property if:

  • The will explicitly grants the power to sell real estate
  • All beneficiaries consent to the sale
  • The court approves the sale (required in some cases)
  • The sale is necessary to pay estate debts or taxes

Many heirs are surprised to learn that waiting until probate closes isn't always necessary — or advisable. If the property is sitting vacant in Titusville, carrying costs like property taxes, insurance, utilities, and lawn maintenance continue to accumulate. In some cases, the estate may not have liquid funds to cover these expenses, forcing a sale earlier rather than later.

The Brevard County Probate Timeline: What to Expect

While every estate is different, here's a realistic timeline for selling an inherited house through probate in Brevard County:

Month 1: Filing and Appointment

The probate process begins when someone files a petition with the Brevard County Clerk of Courts in Viera. If there's a will, it must be submitted. The court will appoint a personal representative, who receives "letters of administration" granting legal authority to act on behalf of the estate. This step alone can take 2 to 6 weeks depending on court backlog.

Month 2–3: Inventory and Creditor Period

The personal representative must inventory all estate assets, including the property in Melbourne, Palm Bay, or wherever it's located. Florida law also requires publishing a notice to creditors, giving them 90 days to file claims against the estate. During this period, the property should be secured, insured, and maintained.

Month 4–6: Property Evaluation and Sale Preparation

Once creditor claims are known, the personal representative can move forward with selling the property. This typically involves:

  • Getting a professional appraisal or comparative market analysis
  • Deciding whether to make repairs or sell as-is
  • Choosing between a traditional listing and a cash buyer
  • Obtaining court approval if required

For homes in older neighborhoods like Cocoa or Cape Canaveral, where properties may need significant updating, this decision is especially important. A traditional sale could require $30,000 to $60,000 in repairs and months of showings. A cash sale can close in as little as two weeks.

Month 7–12: Closing and Distribution

After the sale closes, proceeds are held by the estate. Debts, taxes, and probate costs are paid first. What's left is distributed to heirs according to the will — or Florida's intestacy laws if there was no will. Only then is probate officially closed.

Florida's Intestacy Laws: What Happens Without a Will

If your loved one died without a will, Florida's intestacy laws determine who inherits the property. This is more common than you might think, and it can complicate the sale significantly:

  • Surviving spouse, no children: Spouse inherits everything.
  • Surviving spouse with children from the marriage: Spouse inherits everything.
  • Surviving spouse with children from a previous relationship: Spouse gets 50%, children share the other 50%.
  • No spouse, but children: Children share equally.
  • No spouse or children: Parents, then siblings, then more distant relatives.

In communities like Merritt Island and Satellite Beach, where many residents are retirees who moved from other states, it's not uncommon to find that a parent's Florida home must be split among adult children living in New York, Ohio, or California. Coordinating a sale across multiple time zones and opinions can be a logistical nightmare — another reason cash buyers, who offer simplicity and speed, are so appealing.

Common Challenges When Selling an Inherited Property in Brevard County

Every inherited home sale has its own complications. In Brevard County, we see several challenges repeatedly:

Outdated or Deteriorated Condition

Many inherited homes haven't been updated in 20 or 30 years. Shag carpet, popcorn ceilings, aluminum wiring, and original windows are common in neighborhoods throughout Viera, Cocoa, and Rockledge. Heirs who live out of state simply don't have the bandwidth to manage contractors and renovations.

Code Violations and Unpermitted Work

Older homes may have additions, garage conversions, or pool enclosures that were never permitted. In Brevard County, unpermitted work can derail a traditional buyer's financing. Cash buyers, however, regularly purchase properties with code issues and handle the remediation themselves.

Hurricane and Water Damage

Florida's harsh coastal climate takes a toll on homes. Inherited properties in Cocoa Beach, Satellite Beach, and Merritt Island may have hidden water intrusion, roof leaks, or mold issues that aren't immediately visible. A pre-listing inspection often reveals expensive surprises.

Title Issues and Liens

Reverse mortgages, unpaid property taxes, HOA liens, and Medicaid estate recovery claims can all attach to inherited property. In 2026, with property values elevated across Brevard County, tax bills have risen accordingly — and some estates don't have the cash to pay overdue taxes.

Family Disagreements

Nothing strains family relationships like an inheritance. One sibling wants to keep the house. Another wants top dollar. A third just wants the cash and doesn't care about sentimental value. When multiple heirs are involved, reaching consensus on listing price, repairs, and timing can be nearly impossible.
