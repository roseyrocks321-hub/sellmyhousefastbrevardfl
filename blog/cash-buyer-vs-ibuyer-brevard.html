Cash Buyer vs. iBuyer in Brevard County: Which Gets You More Money?

If you’re thinking about selling your house quickly in Brevard County, you’ve probably encountered two seemingly similar options: a local cash buyer and an iBuyer. On the surface, both promise speed, convenience, and a streamlined process without the headaches of a traditional listing. But beneath the marketing gloss, these two paths are dramatically different — and choosing the wrong one can cost you tens of thousands of dollars.

In this guide, we’ll pull back the curtain on what iBuyers really are, how they operate in markets like Melbourne, Palm Bay, and Viera, the hidden fees and repair deductions that eat into your bottom line, and why a local cash home buyer often delivers more money, more flexibility, and more peace of mind for homeowners across the Space Coast.

What Is an iBuyer?

The term “iBuyer” stands for instant buyer — a national or regional company that uses automated valuation models (AVMs) and algorithms to make quick cash offers on homes, often without ever setting foot inside the property. Companies like Opendoor, Offerpad, and RedfinNow pioneered the iBuyer model, promising homeowners the ability to sell online in a matter of days with minimal disruption.

Here’s how the typical iBuyer process works:

  • You enter your address and basic property details on the iBuyer’s website.
  • Their algorithm generates an offer, usually within 24 to 48 hours.
  • If you accept, you choose a closing date, typically within 30 to 60 days.
  • An iBuyer representative conducts a brief inspection to assess condition.
  • The company adjusts the final price based on needed repairs and their own cost calculations.

It sounds seamless, and for some sellers in hot markets with pristine homes, it can be. But for the average homeowner in Cocoa Beach, Rockledge, Titusville, or Satellite Beach, the reality is often far less appealing once the final numbers are tallied.

The True Cost of Selling to an iBuyer

iBuyers advertise convenience, but that convenience comes at a steep price. Many homeowners are shocked to discover that their net proceeds from an iBuyer sale are significantly lower than expected. Here’s where the money disappears.

Service Fees of 5% to 7% (or More)

Unlike a traditional real estate agent who charges a commission, iBuyers charge a service fee that typically ranges from 5% to 7% of the sale price. Some iBuyers have pushed fees even higher during market corrections. This fee is non-negotiable and covers the iBuyer’s costs of holding, marketing, and reselling your home. On a $350,000 house in Merritt Island, a 6% service fee amounts to $21,000 — before a single repair is deducted.

Repair Deductions That Sting

After you accept the initial offer, the iBuyer sends an inspector. The resulting repair estimate can be eye-watering. Because iBuyers aim to resell the home quickly and in move-in-ready condition, they often demand credit for issues a traditional buyer might overlook or negotiate flexibly. We’ve seen repair deductions range from $5,000 to $30,000 or more on Brevard County homes — for issues as minor as outdated fixtures, worn carpet, or an aging HVAC system.

Consider this scenario: A homeowner in Palm Bay receives an iBuyer offer of $320,000. After the 6% service fee ($19,200) and repair deductions ($12,000), the net offer drops to $288,800. That’s over $31,000 in costs the seller never anticipated when they first saw the headline number.

Below-Market Offers

iBuyers are not charities. They are large corporations with shareholders, overhead, and profit margins to protect. Their algorithm-generated offers are designed to be conservative — low enough to guarantee a profit when they resell, but high enough to attract sellers who prioritize convenience. In practice, this means iBuyer offers are typically 5% to 15% below true market value before fees and repairs are even applied.

The Local Cash Buyer Alternative

A local cash buyer is typically a real estate investor or investment company based in or near Brevard County who purchases homes directly from homeowners for cash. Unlike iBuyers, local cash buyers evaluate each property individually, understand the nuances of the Space Coast market, and can offer terms tailored to your specific situation.

Market Knowledge That Matters

Local cash buyers know that a home in Viera near the A-rated schools commands a different value than a similar-sized home in Cocoa needing updates. They understand how Cape Canaveral tourism affects rental demand, how Melbourne tech workers are reshaping neighborhoods, and where Titusville is gentrifying fastest. This granular knowledge leads to more accurate, more competitive offers — not algorithmic guesses based on county-wide averages.

No Hidden Fees or Commissions

When you sell to a reputable local cash buyer, there are no realtor commissions, no service fees, and no closing costs paid by you. The offer you receive is the amount you walk away with at closing, minus any outstanding mortgage or liens. This transparency eliminates the nasty surprises that plague iBuyer transactions.

Flexibility on Timeline and Terms

iBuyers operate on rigid timelines dictated by corporate calendars and resale targets. A local cash buyer can adapt to your needs. Need to close in 7 days because of a foreclosure auction? We can do that. Need 60 days to find your next home in Satellite Beach? We can do that too. Need to leave unwanted furniture behind? No problem. This flexibility is impossible to find with a national iBuyer algorithm.

Buying As-Is — Really As-Is

While iBuyers deduct for repairs after inspection, most local cash buyers make their offer based on the property’s current condition and do not renegotiate later. If your Rockledge home has a leaking roof, foundation cracks, or inherited clutter from decades of accumulation, a local cash buyer factors those issues into the initial offer — no deductions, no surprises.

When Does an iBuyer Make Sense?

To be fair, iBuyers serve a purpose in the market. For homeowners with a nearly new home in a cookie-cutter subdivision, minimal repairs, and a high tolerance for fees, an iBuyer might offer the lowest-effort path to sale. If you live in a brand-new Viera development and value convenience above all else, exploring an iBuyer offer is not unreasonable — as long as you read every line of the contract and budget for deductions.

However, for the vast majority of Brevard County homeowners — especially those with older homes, inherited properties, financial distress, or unique circumstances — a local cash buyer is the smarter financial choice.

You’re a neighbor.

How to Evaluate Any Cash Offer

Whether you choose an iBuyer or a local cash buyer, protect yourself by asking these questions before signing:

  • What is the net amount I will receive after all fees, commissions, and deductions?
  • Are there any contingencies that allow the buyer to reduce the price after inspection?
  • Who pays closing costs, and what are they estimated to be?
  • Can I choose my closing date, and is there flexibility if my plans change?
  • What happens if title issues or liens are discovered?
  • Can you provide references from recent sellers in Brevard County?

A reputable local buyer will answer these questions clearly and confidently. If a buyer is evasive or pressures you to sign quickly, walk away.
