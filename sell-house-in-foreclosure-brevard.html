Can You Sell a House in Foreclosure in Brevard County? Here's How

If you've received a foreclosure notice and you're wondering whether it's too late to save your home, here's the answer: Yes, you can sell a house in foreclosure in Brevard County — and in many cases, selling before the auction is the smartest financial move you can make.

Every year, dozens of homeowners across Melbourne, Palm Bay, Cocoa Beach, Titusville, and the rest of the Space Coast lose their properties to foreclosure auctions — often because they didn't realize they had options. This guide explains how the Florida foreclosure timeline works, why selling before auction protects your equity, and how a cash buyer can help you close in time.

The Florida Foreclosure Timeline: Where Are You?

Florida is a judicial foreclosure state, meaning lenders must file a lawsuit and obtain court approval before they can auction your property. While this makes the process longer than in non-judicial states, it also creates a window of opportunity for homeowners who act quickly.

Pre-Foreclosure: Days 1–120

The pre-foreclosure period begins when you miss your first mortgage payment and ends when the lender files a lis pendens in court. During this phase, your loan is delinquent but the legal process hasn't officially started. This is the best time to explore your options, because you have the most flexibility and the least pressure.

If you're in pre-foreclosure, you can:

  • Negotiate a loan modification or forbearance with your lender
  • Refinance if you still qualify
  • List the home traditionally with a realtor
  • Accept a cash offer and close quickly

Active Foreclosure: Days 120–180+

Once the lender files the foreclosure lawsuit and serves you with a complaint, the case enters active litigation. You'll have 20 days to respond. If you don't, the court may enter a default judgment. If you do respond, the case may proceed through discovery, mediation, and eventually a summary judgment hearing.

Even during active foreclosure, you retain the right to sell the property. The sale proceeds will be used to pay off the mortgage, legal fees, and any other liens, with remaining equity going to you. The key is to sell before the court sets an auction date.

Pre-Auction: The Final Countdown

Once the court grants a final judgment of foreclosure, a sale date is scheduled — usually within 30 to 60 days. In Brevard County, foreclosure auctions are typically held online or at the courthouse in Viera. At this stage, time is running out, but a sale is still possible if you can close before the auction.

Post-Auction: Redemption and Deficiency

After the auction, Florida law provides a brief period during which you may be able to challenge the sale or, in limited cases, redeem the property by paying the full judgment amount. However, redemption is rare and requires substantial cash. Most homeowners who reach this stage have few practical options left.

Why Selling Before Auction Protects Your Equity

When a home sells at foreclosure auction, it rarely fetches market value. Auction buyers are investors looking for deep discounts, and the lender can bid the amount of the outstanding loan — which may be far less than what the home is worth. Any equity you built over years of mortgage payments can disappear in minutes.

Selling before auction allows you to:

  • Control the sale price: You can market the home to a broad pool of buyers or accept a fair cash offer based on current market conditions.
  • Preserve your credit: A completed foreclosure stays on your credit report for seven years and can drop your score by 100 points or more. A sale, even a short sale, does far less damage.
  • Avoid deficiency judgments: If the auction sale doesn't cover the full loan balance, Florida law allows lenders to pursue a deficiency judgment against you for the difference. Selling at market value reduces or eliminates this risk.
  • Minimize legal fees: Every month the foreclosure drags on, attorney fees and court costs accumulate — costs that ultimately come out of any equity you have.

If you're facing foreclosure, time is your enemy. A traditional listing takes 30–60 days on average — not counting the time needed for repairs, staging, and buyer financing. By the time you find a qualified buyer, the auction may have already happened.

Once closing is complete, the title company pays off your mortgage and any other liens. The foreclosure case is dismissed, and any remaining proceeds go to you. The entire process can take as little as 7 to 14 days from the time you accept the offer.

Steps to Sell Your Home Before Foreclosure Auction

If you're ready to sell, follow these steps to maximize your chances of success:

  1. Gather your documents. Collect your mortgage statement, foreclosure complaint, correspondence from your lender, and any court notices. These will help the buyer understand the timeline and payoff amount.
  2. Know your numbers. Find out exactly how much you owe, including principal, interest, late fees, and legal costs. You can request a payoff statement from your lender.
  3. Estimate your equity. Use online tools or a comparative market analysis to estimate your home's value. If you owe $200,000 and the home is worth $280,000, you have $80,000 in equity to protect.
  4. Contact multiple buyers. Get at least two cash offers to ensure you're receiving fair market value. Be wary of anyone who pressures you to sign immediately.
  5. Notify your lender. Let your lender know you're pursuing a sale. Some lenders will pause foreclosure proceedings or extend deadlines if they believe a sale is imminent.
  6. Stay in communication. Respond promptly to requests from the buyer, title company, and your attorney. Delays at this stage can be costly.

What If You Owe More Than the Home Is Worth?

If your mortgage balance exceeds the home's current value, you're in a negative equity position — also known as being "underwater." In this case, a traditional sale won't generate enough to pay off the loan, but you still have options.

Short Sale

In a short sale, your lender agrees to accept less than the full loan balance as payment in full. Short sales require lender approval and can take several months, which may not be feasible if the auction is imminent. However, if you're early in the foreclosure process, a short sale may be worth pursuing.

Deed in Lieu of Foreclosure

A deed in lieu allows you to transfer ownership to the lender voluntarily, avoiding the auction entirely. This option is usually only available if there are no other liens on the property and the lender agrees to waive any deficiency.

Bankruptcy

Filing for Chapter 13 bankruptcy can halt foreclosure through an automatic stay, giving you time to catch up on payments or negotiate a sale. Chapter 7 may delay foreclosure but typically won't save the home. Consult a bankruptcy attorney before taking this step.

Local Resources for Brevard County Homeowners in Foreclosure

You don't have to face foreclosure alone. These local and state resources can help:

  • Brevard County Clerk of Courts: Access foreclosure records, auction schedules, and case filings online or at the Viera courthouse.
  • HUD-Approved Housing Counselors: Free counseling services to help you understand your mortgage relief options and negotiate with your lender.
  • Legal Aid of Brevard County: Provides free or low-cost legal assistance to qualifying homeowners facing foreclosure.
  • Florida Hardest-Hit Fund: State programs that may provide mortgage assistance to unemployed or underemployed homeowners.
