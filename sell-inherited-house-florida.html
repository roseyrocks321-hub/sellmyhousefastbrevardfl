Inheriting a House in Florida: What You Need to Know

Inheriting a house in Florida can feel like both a blessing and a burden. Between probate court, property taxes, maintenance costs, insurance, and family dynamics, many heirs find themselves overwhelmed — especially if they live out of state or the property needs significant repairs.

The good news: you have options. And if you need to sell quickly, Brevard Cash Buyers can purchase the property as-is, even while it's still in probate, so you can settle the estate and move forward.

The Florida Probate Process

Before you can sell an inherited property in Florida, the estate typically needs to go through probate — the legal process of distributing a deceased person's assets. Here's what to expect:

Summary Administration

If the estate is valued under $75,000 (or the decedent has been deceased for more than 2 years), the estate may qualify for Summary Administration. This is a faster, simplified probate process that typically takes 2 to 3 months in Brevard County.

Formal Administration

For larger or more complex estates, Formal Administration is required. This process involves appointing a personal representative, notifying creditors, and court oversight. It typically takes 6 to 12 months in Florida.

Can you sell before probate is complete? In many cases, yes. If you're the appointed personal representative (executor), you can accept a cash offer and set a closing date that aligns with the probate timeline. We work with probate attorneys regularly and can structure the purchase to fit your situation.

Tax Benefits: Stepped-Up Basis

One major advantage of inheriting property in Florida is the stepped-up basis tax rule. When you inherit a house, its tax basis is "stepped up" to its fair market value at the time of the previous owner's death.

What does this mean for you? If the house was worth $300,000 when you inherited it and you sell it for $300,000, you owe zero capital gains tax. This can save you tens of thousands compared to if the original owner had sold it themselves.

Common Challenges When Selling an Inherited House

  • Out-of-state heirs: Managing a property from another state is stressful and expensive.
  • Multiple heirs: Disagreements among siblings or family members can stall decisions for months.
  • Property condition: Older homes often need repairs, updates, or have code violations.
  • Hoarding or neglect: Many inherited homes are full of belongings and haven't been maintained.
  • Ongoing costs: Property taxes, insurance, utilities, and HOA fees keep adding up.
  • Emotional weight: Sorting through a loved one's home is emotionally draining.
